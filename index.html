2 · Why the answers differ

Each market grades the same change on its own scale

Every authority publishes its own list of categories under its own names. What actually travels between them is not the name. It is how much permission the category demands before you are allowed to act.

Just do it. Nobody needs telling. pqs_no_filing
Do it, and mention it in the yearly bundle. do_and_document
Do it, and file it the same day. notify_at_implementation
File it, sit out the waiting period, then do it. notify_then_wait
Ask, wait for a yes, then do it. prior_approval
off the scale This is not a change to the licence. It needs a new one. new_application
off the scale It depends on a deal struck earlier, not on this change. content_dependent

Hover or tap a rung to see which licences landed on it. Five rungs can be ranked, so the strictest market can be named before the other forty are done. The bottom two are not rungs, they are exits, and they are counted on their own. That is the whole reason the first list could say Brazil governs.

3 · The modelling problem

A rule book and a place are not the same thing

The source material files everything under one column called jurisdiction. Treat that as a list of countries and three ordinary cases stop working.

A book with no country

Eleven nations share one regional guideline, and you cannot file anything against it. Every filing goes to a national authority instead.

One country, two books

Brazil grades chemical and biological medicines under separate rules. The product decides the book, which is why Brazil appears twice in the first list.

One book, three licences

A single European licence can cover thirty countries, or those countries can hold thirty separate licences. All of them grade against one book.

So the model keeps the book and the place apart, and the licence says which book governs it. That one decision is what makes all three cases ordinary instead of special.

4 · What it stores

Four questions, and what answers each one

Where is this product licensed? Registration → Market A licence, its number, and whether it is still in force. The thing a change actually hits, and one filing each.
Whose rules apply there? RuleSet A book of categories, which markets use it, which kinds of product it covers, and the date it took effect.
What can the answer be? ReportingCategory One entry in one book, carrying the authority's own name for it and where it sits on the scale above.
What did we decide, and does it still hold? ChangeClassification The answer for one licence, with who decided it, when, why, and which edition of the book they used.

Five models in total, counting the change itself. The brief asked for the fewest concepts that do the job, and for every field to be read by something.

5 · The part nobody asked for

An answer can rot while you are not looking

9 Jan 2026 Europe assessed and written down
15 Jan 2026 The European guideline is replaced

Six days apart. Nobody made a mistake and nothing about the change moved, but the reasoning in that row now sits against a retired edition. A spreadsheet cell would look exactly as confident as it did on the ninth.

So every answer records which edition it was decided against, who decided it and when. Whether it still holds is worked out fresh each time you look, never stored, so it can never disagree with the facts it is summarising. Licences lapse too, which is why Singapore is flagged above.

One thing moving is enough. No scores and no percentages, because the rules are yes-or-no and nobody can file a 73 percent confidence.

6 · What it deliberately cannot do

It records the decision. It does not make it.

Every market publishes its list of categories. None of them, in the material provided, publishes the index saying which kind of change lands in which category. So a qualified person makes the call, and this holds it, keeps track of which licences still need one, and says when a recorded call has been undermined.

Also left out on purpose: what is actually registered in each market, which some limits are measured against, and grouped filings, where one change matches several entries at once. Both are named in the memo with what would make them worth building.
